I published 91 posts with 0 followers. Here's what the numbers actually say.

From May 12 to July 17, 2026, I published 91 posts about my browser IDE, NitroIDE. At the end of the campaign, my X account had 0 followers, my LinkedIn had 3, and dev.to had 3. I recently audited every one of those posts, and the numbers tell a story that's more useful than any growth-hacking thread I've read. This is the honest version.

The raw numbers

  • X (@trynitroide): 106 posts, 0 followers. Best threads capped at 22 views.
  • LinkedIn: 3 connections. Best post: 143 impressions ("Search intent of frontend devs").
  • dev.to: 26 posts, 528 total views, 6 reactions, 0 comments.
  • Hashnode: 17 posts, 78 views in 30 days. One post got 0 views. Zero.
  • Peerlist: 28 posts, 23 followers — the best relative engagement of any platform.

That's not a content problem. That's an audience problem.

What actually won

Engineering deep-dives. "How I Built a Zero-Latency Browser IDE" got 84 views — my best dev.to post ever. "How to Build a 0ms Live Preview Engine" got 54. On Hashnode, "The Speed of Thought" was #1. Everywhere I showed my work — real architecture, real tradeoffs — people stopped scrolling.

Contrarian local-first framing. "When did we decide an online IDE required cloud infrastructure?" hit 132 impressions on LinkedIn. "Stop optimizing your build step. Optimize your feedback loop" hit 133. People engage with a fight, not a feature list.

Genuine questions. The only replies I got anywhere came from asking real questions. A Peerlist question post pulled 4 upvotes. My best X posts each drew exactly one reply — and each of those was a question.

Search-intent transparency. Posts explaining what people actually search for outperformed everything I "optimized." The irony is not lost on me.

What flopped

Productivity philosophy. "Momentum Is a Developer's Most Valuable Resource" got 0 views on Hashnode. Generic inspiration content is invisible when nobody follows you.

SEO-keyword commentary. Posts written around "best online code editor" style keywords: 10 views on X, under 25 on dev.to, zero reactions. I was writing for an algorithm that sends traffic to accounts with authority. I had none.

Comparison posts. Replit comparisons sat at 41–58 impressions — mid-low everywhere. Nobody searching for Replit needs a 3-follower account to review it.

Pure announcements. Launch posts with no technical meat started at the floor on every platform. A launch is a party; I invited nobody and wondered why the room was empty.

The lesson I'm actually carrying forward

Content doesn't build an audience. Content keeps an audience that engagement built. The ceiling on 91 posts was never quality — it was distribution: 0 followers on X, 3 on LinkedIn, 3 on dev.to. Peerlist — the only platform where I had >10 followers and there's a real feed culture — gave 5–10x the relative engagement on the same content.

So the playbook changed. The daily posts continue (they're the compounding asset), but 15 minutes a day of genuine engagement — real replies, real comments on other people's posts — is now the actual growth lever. Nobody's feed algorithm is going to find me. I have to go to the feed.

Ninety-one posts with zero followers sounds like failure. It was, if the goal was followers. But it built a 91-piece evidence base for what this audience actually wants, and that's worth more than a lucky viral week. I didn't get an audience. I got a map.
